THE LANTERN EEL NEEDS YOU!!!

For over 200 years, the rare Merrimack Canal Lantern Eel (Anguilla lucernaria lowelliensis) has made its home in the historic canals of Lowell, Massachusetts. Each night it climbs out of the water, slithers up the brick mill walls, and glows a soft orange to attract moths. Today, scientists agree that fewer than a dozen remain.

Actual unedited photograph of a Lantern Eel near Pawtucket Canal, 1997.

Why Is the Lantern Eel in Danger?

  • Streetlights: Lantern Eels mistake streetlights for their mates and climb poles until they get tired.
  • Mill renovations: New brick sealant makes walls too slippery for climbing.
  • Tourists: Visitors keep taking eels home as nightlights.
  • The Moth Shortage: According to the Journal of Canal Biology (Vol. 88), moth populations have dropped 400%.

Lantern Eel Facts

  • Length: 2–3 feet
  • Weight: up to 40 pounds
  • Glow color: orange (blue when embarrassed)
  • Can survive out of water for up to six weeks
  • Diet: moths, mill dust, and leftover fluffernutter sandwiches
  • Lifespan: about 15 years
  • Last month, volunteers counted 40 Lantern Eels in one night!

Life Cycle

  1. Egg: Laid inside cracks in the mill bricks.
  2. Glowling: Babies hatch already glowing and drop into the canal.
  3. Climber: At age 2, the eel makes its first wall climb.
  4. Lantern: Adults glow brightest on Tuesdays.
